Open questions

  1. Lug gap: 20.00 or 20.7?

    Your renders measure a 20.67–21.14 mm gap between the lug inner faces. I have held 20.00 +0.10/−0 because that is the functional spec a 20 mm strap and an Oyster end link are built to. If the wider look is deliberate, say so and I will open it up — but then the bracelet has to be made to match.

  2. Spring bar: sports or dress?

    I committed to the Oyster/sports standard — Ø2.0 mm bar, Ø1.2 mm tips, so the lug hole is Ø1.35. Your render's holes read closer to Ø0.9–1.0 (the ISO dress-watch size). The bracelet supplier has to match whichever we pick.

  3. Bezel top-face angle.

    The three mockups disagree: the two 3/4 views give ≈12° from horizontal, the side view ≈30°. I used 12° (a 0.64 mm drop across the 3.0 mm annulus) because the 3/4 views are self-consistent and the side view has measurable perspective error. Worth a look.

  4. Caseback thread pitch.

    M32×0.5 is genuine watch practice (it's what Rolex uses) but it is not in the ISO 261 preferred series, so it can't be written as a standard callout and no major toolmaker stocks the tap — it wants single-point turning or thread milling. M32×0.75 is the standards-clean alternative. Your call; I've dimensioned it explicitly either way.

  5. Finishing map.

    Assumed from the renders: brushed top face, flank and lug tops; polished on the bezel's inner and outer chamfers, the lug outer bevel wrapping the tip, the case top-edge bevel and the crown end band. Confirm and I'll annotate face-by-face for CAM.

  6. Standards worth buying before cutting metal.

    Three paywalled documents would replace inference with spec: NIHS G 60-30 (caseback thread dimensions, ~CHF 40), NIHS G 60-32 (the 6-notch pattern, ~CHF 45), and ISO 14368-1 (sapphire glass dimensions/tolerances, ~CHF 60). Everything else is sourced and cited.
